Spot Job Scams

A job search can make people vulnerable to pressure. A real opportunity should still stand up to basic verification.

Paying money to “secure” a job is a major red flag

The Department of Employment and Labour has warned about fraudulent job offers that request fees for employment processing or training and may ask for personal or banking details.

Red flags
  • You are asked to pay an application, placement, processing or training fee
  • The offer arrives without a real interview or application
  • The email domain does not match the company
  • The recruiter refuses to let you verify the role independently
  • You are pressured to act immediately
  • The message asks for banking details or ID information unusually early
  • The job description is vague but promises unusually high pay
How to verify
  • Find the company website yourself rather than only using the supplied link
  • Check whether the vacancy appears on the company’s official careers page
  • Call a published company number if you are uncertain
  • Search the recruiter and company separately
  • Do not send money because someone says the vacancy will disappear
  • Be cautious with copies of identity and banking documents
If something feels wrong:

Stop communicating until you verify it. A genuine recruiter should understand reasonable questions about who they are and which company they represent.

Official reference: Department of Employment and Labour warning on fraudulent job opportunities. Last content review: September 2026.
