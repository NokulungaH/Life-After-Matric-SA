PAYE & UIF Explained

Two of the most common payroll deductions in South Africa are PAYE and UIF. They are not the same thing.

OverviewPayslipPAYE & UIFDeductionsOvertime & bonuses

PAYE

PAYE is employees’ tax withheld by an employer and paid over to SARS. The amount depends on taxable remuneration and the applicable tax rules for the tax year.

UIF

UIF is a social-insurance contribution for qualifying workers. Employees generally contribute 1% of remuneration up to the legislated ceiling, with employers contributing as well.

Why your PAYE can differ

Payroll tax can be affected by remuneration structure, retirement contributions, fringe benefits, medical tax credits and other factors. A simple online estimate will not always match payroll exactly.

What to check

Look for PAYE and UIF on your payslip and compare year-to-date figures over time. If you think something is wrong, start with payroll or HR and keep written records of the query.

Official references: SARS individual income tax rates and SARS UIF guidance. Tax rules can change by tax year.

Fictional example · Thando

PAYE and UIF are doing different jobs

On a R18 000 monthly salary, UIF is capped by the UIF earnings ceiling, while PAYE is calculated using income-tax rules. That is why one deduction can stay almost the same while another changes when salary changes.

Key idea: PAYE is income tax collected through payroll. UIF is a separate social-insurance contribution.
