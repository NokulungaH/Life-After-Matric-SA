Adulting admin

Privacy, OTPs & Everyday Scams

A large part of adult admin is learning when not to hand over information.

OverviewDocumentsBankingInsuranceConsumerWorkplaceWillsPrivacy

OTPs and passwords

A legitimate bank or service should not need you to read out a password or one-time PIN so they can 'secure' your account. Treat OTPs like keys.

Identity documents

Ask why a copy is needed and who will receive it. Where appropriate, watermark copies with the purpose and date before sending them.

Links and calls

Do not use the contact details supplied by a suspicious message to verify that same message. Find the organisation’s official contact channel yourself.

Urgency is a tactic

Scams often create panic: your account will close, a parcel will be lost, a job will disappear or a payment must happen immediately. Slow down and verify independently.

Fictional example · Lerato

“Fraud department” asks for an OTP

A caller already knows Lerato’s name and bank. They say they are stopping a suspicious payment and ask her to read out the OTP that just arrived.

Key idea: Knowing personal details does not prove the caller is legitimate. Lerato should end the call and contact the bank using its official number or app.
