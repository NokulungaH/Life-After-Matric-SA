Build a Strong CV

Your CV does not need to be fancy. It needs to make it easy for an employer to understand what you can do.

Overview CV Applying Interviews Contracts Job scams First job
01

Start with the essentials

Use your name, mobile number, professional email address and city or area. You usually do not need to put your ID number, marital status, religion, race or a photo on a general CV unless there is a clear and legitimate reason for it.

02

Use a short profile

Two or three lines are enough. Say what you are, what you are good at and what kind of opportunity you are looking for. Avoid empty phrases like “hard-working team player” unless you back them up elsewhere.

03

Put the most relevant evidence first

If you have work experience, lead with your most relevant recent roles. If you are a graduate with little experience, projects, practical assignments, volunteering, internships and technical skills can carry more weight.

A clean CV order
  1. Name and contact details
  2. Short professional profile
  3. Skills relevant to the role
  4. Work experience / internships / projects
  5. Education and qualifications
  6. Certifications if relevant
  7. References: “Available on request” is fine unless requested upfront
Common mistakes
  • Sending the same CV to every job
  • Spelling mistakes in company or tool names
  • Long paragraphs instead of scannable bullets
  • Listing duties without showing outcomes
  • Using an unprofessional email address
  • Including sensitive personal information unnecessarily
Better bullet example

Instead of: “Responsible for customer queries.”

Try: “Resolved customer queries by phone and email and escalated complex cases to the correct team.”
