Contracts & Consumer Basics

Do not treat 'terms and conditions' as decoration. They are often the rules you will be held to later.

OverviewDocumentsBankingInsuranceConsumerWorkplaceWillsPrivacy

Before signing

Read price, duration, cancellation, automatic renewal, penalties and what the supplier promises to provide.

Keep evidence

Save contracts, invoices, screenshots of important offers and written complaints. A clear timeline helps when a dispute arises.

Escalate in writing

Start with the supplier’s complaints process and keep reference numbers. If unresolved, the correct regulator or ombud depends on the industry.

Avoid panic decisions

High-pressure sales tactics are a reason to slow down. Do not sign or pay because someone claims the offer expires in minutes.

Fictional example · Thando

The appliance stops working

Thando buys a new microwave and it fails shortly afterward. Instead of throwing away the receipt or arguing only by phone, he keeps proof of purchase, writes down the fault and uses the retailer’s formal complaints process.

Key idea: Good records make consumer complaints much easier to escalate if the first conversation does not solve the problem.
