Education & career

Internships

An internship can help you turn theory into workplace evidence, but not every internship is equally useful.

OverviewUniversityTVETLearnershipsFundingInternshipsUpskilling

Look for real learning

A useful internship should expose you to meaningful work, supervision, feedback and skills you can later describe on your CV.

Clarify the basics

Check duration, hours, location, stipend or salary, expected duties and whether there is any realistic pathway after the programme.

Document what you do

Keep notes on projects, tools, outcomes and responsibilities so you can later turn the experience into strong CV bullets and interview examples.

Fictional example · Ayanda

Turning internship work into CV evidence

Weak CV line: “Helped with admin.”

Stronger: “Captured and checked weekly customer records in Excel and prepared summary reports for the operations team.”

Key idea: The internship becomes more valuable when Ayanda can explain what she actually did, with which tools, and for what purpose.
